## 3. Outcome-sensitive criticality definition

A conflict is critical if selecting any one of the preserved conflicting versions can change at least one of these five classes:

1. Eligibility result;
2. any of the four Qualification results;
3. compliance with a Hard Constraint or mandatory Architecture §18.1 condition;
4. protected-attribute/proxy, lawful-basis, authority or another legal/rights boundary;
5. admissibility of Safe Presentation or subsequent disclosure.

This is a qualitative outcome-sensitivity definition. It is not a numeric severity score, critical-field catalog, classifier, legal verdict or permission to choose one conflicting version as truth.

## 4. Qualification precedence and human review

A critical conflict requires `HUMAN_REVIEW_REQUIRED` subject to the complete `XFR-D-033` precedence hierarchy. It does not override confirmed Eligibility `INELIGIBLE` with the required evidence, does not delete other applicable causes and does not approve queue, reviewer, RBAC, verdict or runtime mechanics.

`XFR-D-033`, `XFR-D-038`, `XFR-D-040`, `XFR-D-041`, `XFR-D-043`, `XFR-D-044`, `XFR-D-045` and applicable evidence-governance decisions remain independent. G48 does not reopen, absorb or resolve them.

## 5. Noncritical conflict boundary

A noncritical conflict still preserves all versions and evidence references, reduces Confidence, is not a negative fact, and by itself creates no automatic rejection. It continues only under separately approved Confidence, completeness and verification rules.

Favorable score, rank, Confidence, business urgency or aggregate evidence cannot erase a critical conflict or replace required human review. Conversely, the mere existence of a noncritical conflict cannot be coerced into a Hard Constraint failure, `INELIGIBLE`, `REJECTED_BY_MATCHING`, adverse legal fact or automatic route.
